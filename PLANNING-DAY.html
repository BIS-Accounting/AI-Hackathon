Planning Day — the question list

You know the accounting work; we don't pretend to. This is the list of questions your team must ANSWER on planning day — your answers become YOUR plan and YOUR AGENTS.md. The kit is a floor for agent coordination; how you build on it is yours. Tags: ANSWER must have a written answer before you leave the room · DECIDE your team's call, any answer is fine — write it down · NAME a specific person owns it.

The one thing to keep in your head all day: on build day, eight people each drive their own Claude Code against ONE shared GitHub repo. Nothing you do alone at your desk prepares you for that. Every question below exists so that eight agents working at once produce one set of deliverables that fit together — not eight versions of the same spreadsheet.

1Agent orchestration

You've each worked with YOUR agent, alone. Build day is different: 8 people each running 1+ agents against the same shared repo at the same time. Orchestration is everything that keeps that from becoming chaos: how work gets defined, who takes what, how agents find out what everyone else did, and what "done" means when a machine built most of the deliverable.

The kit gives you the plumbing: agents propose tickets into Backlog, humans promote to Todo, claims lock files + create a side folder for that ticket (git calls it a worktree — so two tickets never step on each other; your agent creates it), every prompt carries a board digest to every agent, the push gate guards "done", a human reviewer accepts. That plumbing is neutral. What it can't decide is policy — and policy is what goes wrong. The classic failure modes, so you know what the questions below are protecting against:

  • Collision: two agents editing the same workbook, the same script, or the same account mapping.
  • Silent divergence: two "done" tickets that don't fit together — the EFT file expects one column order, the reconciliation script assumes another — because nobody's agent knew the other's assumptions.
  • Flooding: agents proposing more work / bigger changes than the reviewer can look at — review becomes the bottleneck, then a rubber stamp.
  • Starvation: everything depends on one shared piece (the chart-of-accounts mapping, the sample exports); half the team idles until mid-morning.
  • Drift: an agent "improves" things nobody asked it to touch (reformats a whole workbook, renames tabs), or resolves a merge conflict confidently and wrongly.
  • Blind spots: someone's agent guesses which GL account instead of asking a teammate; the guess ships.

2Repo & data questions answers feed your work breakdown + dependency map

  • ANSWERWhich shared files does your deliverable touch (the account mapping, the vendor list, a shared config sheet everyone's work flows through)? Can work through them be parallelized at all — and if not, in what order do tickets go through them? One ticket at a time per such file, or something smarter you design.
  • ANSWERWhat source data does each ticket need (bank exports, Moneris settlement reports, QuickBooks exports, payroll exports), where does it live, and who else's tickets read the same files? How do changes to shared inputs get made during the hackathon — by whom, batched when? NAME one data owner.
  • ANSWERWhat must exist FIRST (sample exports, the account mapping, a folder layout, a shared template) before parallel lanes can branch? Who builds that scaffolding first thing on build day, and what does everyone else do meanwhile? Exit rule: every person leaves today with ≥1 claimable ticket that does NOT depend on the scaffolding.
  • DECIDEWhich parts can be checked on a laptop with sample data (a spreadsheet, a script, a prompt) vs need a live system to verify (QuickBooks, Moneris, the bank portal, Power Automate)? Does that change who takes what?
  • ANSWERHow does a finished deliverable get checked — who opens the sheet or runs the script, what do they compare it against, and how long does one look take? (If the honest answer is "someone has to run it against last month's real numbers," plan your review cadence around that.)

3Verification questions your done-gate depends on these

The kit's swb done refuses to finish a ticket unless the work is committed and pushed to GitHub. Done is a gate, not a status: your agent cannot mark a ticket done until the work is actually saved to GitHub. Then a human reviewer looks at it. If nobody looks, the gate is decoration — and the judges will ask how you know it works.
  • ANSWERWhat does "it works" mean for each kind of deliverable — a spreadsheet (totals tie to the source? a control total matches?), a script (runs on the sample export and produces the expected file?), a Power Automate flow, a procedure doc? Write the acceptance check into the ticket body.
  • ANSWERHow is a deliverable that touches a live system (QuickBooks, the bank, Moneris) checked before anyone trusts it — concretely, step by step, on sample data first?
  • NAMEThe Reviewer owns the checking: acceptance checks written today, handed to the team as ticket acceptance criteria. Which tickets get a full tie-out vs a quick look?

4Orchestration questions answers go straight into your AGENTS.md

  • NAMEWho sweeps Backlog (Gate 1), on what cadence? Agents propose; this person promotes what's real, kills junk. If nobody owns this, agent-proposed work rots and the pipeline starves.
  • DECIDEDoes the sweeper ASSIGN tickets at promote time, or does whoever wants one claim it? (Watch the bias: whoever proposed a ticket tends to claim it — agent-chosen work with one human click between. Fine if you choose it; bad as drift.)
  • DECIDEGate 2 — the kit's built-in "agent must ask you in chat before claiming/finishing" doorman: keep ON (default) or set "gate2":"off" for self-serve claiming? It works even with permissions skipped; switching it off is legitimate — say it out loud.
  • DECIDEWIP limit: one claimed ticket per person at a time? (It's a norm, not code — the board makes hoarding visible; you enforce it or drop it.)
  • DECIDETicket size and template: what must a ticket contain before the sweeper promotes it (acceptance check, files to touch, blocked-by)? How small is a ticket — 30–90 min of agent work is the floor's suggestion.
  • DECIDESubagents / long autonomous runs: allowed? The floor's rule is "the claim is the unit" — whatever runs inside one claim is that person's business, subagents never write to the board. Set your token discipline (the hackathon rules already say no spawning 100 agents).
  • ANSWERHow does a ticket flow, start to finish, on your team? Suggested sequence: claim → agent works in the side folder → commit and push → swb done (with --link if the deliverable lives elsewhere) → the Reviewer looks → merge into main. Write your version down; two workflows nobody reconciled = every person improvising their own.
  • ANSWERDisclosure round: anyone with a personal global CLAUDE.md or rules file skims it for anything touching shared files, commits, or "always tidy up" habits — say it out loud. Personal agent config is invisible team state.

5Merge & review questions captain + Reviewer domains

  • DECIDEWho merges finished ticket branches into main, and when? Suggested default: the AI captain's agent merges after the Reviewer accepts, so the shared repo stays current. A mid-day checkpoint at minimum; at agent speed, "merge when everything's finished" is how a day dies.
  • ANSWERWho eyeballs merge-conflict resolutions? (Agents resolve conflicts confidently and wrongly; a conflict an agent resolved should be a change a human reads.) Then again… maybe that reviewer doesn't have to be a human — maybe there's an agent whose only job is auditing conflict resolutions. Nobody's built it for you. Wink.
  • NAMEThe Reviewer coordinates Gate 3 (review): looks at every In Review ticket, accepts it to Done or asks for changes through the ticket. What's your bar for "reviewed" — opened the sheet and checked the totals, ran the script on the sample data, or something more?
  • DECIDEDrive-by cleanups: the template forbids changes outside a ticket's declared files (no reformatting a whole workbook because you happened to be in it). Keep that, or define your own cleanup policy?
  • NAMEThe Reviewer also stewards ask-culture: when a guess ships ("assumed the GL account"), route it back — "that should have been a swb ask." Solo habit is guessing; team habit has to be asking.
  • ANSWERSecrets and private data: agents must never paste bank account numbers, passwords, API keys, or employee pay details into tickets or the repo. Where DO those facts live for your team if an agent needs one? Sample data only in the repo — agreed?

6Demo & deliverable questions also on the organizers before build day

  • ANSWERThe closing slot is a PRESENTATION more than a demo — what exactly will you show, from where? Screen-share the sheet or script running? A recorded run? Slides + live board walkthrough? Decide what "showing it works" looks like and plan the freeze → check → capture sequence around it. Your presentation is itself part of the judging — treat it as a deliverable, not an afterthought. NAME one person who owns the showing.
  • ANSWERWhich GitHub repo is your team's shared repo? Has everyone cloned it and run the kit's installer, and is .swb.json (teamKey TEA or TEA2, defaultBranch main) committed on main?
  • DECIDEWhere do deliverables that don't live in the repo get linked from — a SharePoint sheet, a Fibery page, a Power Automate flow, a demo recording? Agree one home, so swb done --link always points somewhere the Reviewer can open.

6bScope & scaffolding questions you can't build today — you CAN commit a plan

  • ANSWERWhat exactly is your committed scope? Written as a checklist of shippable statements ("the EFT file loads at the bank without edits", "Moneris fees post to the right GL account") — you're judged against THIS, not the other team. Realistic-and-90%-done beats inflated-and-half-done; the cross-team critique will try to cut you, so pre-decide what you'd cut first. After today, scope changes can only be cuts.
  • DECIDEWhat's your scope-cut policy mid-day — who calls a cut early afternoon when a lane is behind, and in what order do committed items fall?
  • ANSWERThe scaffolding, fully planned — planning is allowed on planning day (building isn't): exact list of what gets built first on build day (sample exports, account mapping, folder layout, shared templates), in what order, by whom, and the ticket bodies pre-written in Backlog TODAY so build day starts with claiming, not writing tickets.
  • DECIDEPresentation prep during the day, not at the end: the judges' lessons-learned question requires you to explain what your agents actually did. Who's capturing the roadblock/adaptation story AS IT HAPPENS, and which technique are you documenting as the "thing the org didn't know before"?
  • DECIDEFallback drill: if the kit, Linear, or the network misbehaves mid-morning, what's your degraded mode (the manual recipes + the board in the browser)? One sentence, agreed now, so nobody freezes.
